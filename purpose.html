our purpose

why selva exists.

most social platforms are built to grow, or to keep you scrolling, or to sell ads against your attention. selva is built to do something smaller and harder: put the right people in a room, publish work worth their time, and give some of the money back. this page is our plain account of all three.

the manifesto

the room, not the feed.

Selva is a private community for creatives and culturally minded people — designed so that everything you see is worth your time. not curated by an algorithm. not ranked by engagement. written and published by editors, read by humans, shared among a small number of people who opted in on purpose.

we built it because the platforms we grew up on are tired. instagram has become a supermarket. linkedin is a theatre. tiktok is a slot machine. the feed format, once a way to stay connected, has become a way to stay exhausted. somewhere in the middle of all that noise, the thing we actually wanted — a small, warm, careful room with people who notice the difference — went missing.

so we built that room. it's an app, technically. it's a magazine, partly. it's a membership, formally. but it's really just a room — a door that takes some effort to walk through, and a few hours a week worth spending inside once you do.

we don't run ads. we don't sell data. we don't rank anything by like count. the business model is straightforward: members pay $33/mo or $333/yr, we use it to pay the editors and the writers and the infrastructure, and a public percentage of the remainder goes to philanthropy — because it should.

— the selva team

what we stand for

five commitments. written plainly.

  • intentionality over virality.

    every piece of content, every member, every product in the marketplace is chosen. nothing is here because the algorithm picked it up.

  • depth over breadth.

    we'd rather serve fewer people, better. the quality gate at the door is real — but the cap is taste, not a number.

  • culture as a living practice.

    art, sound, wellness, mind, entrepreneurship, nature. these aren't categories. they are ways of paying attention.

  • quiet confidence.

    we don't shout. we don't chase trends. we don't explain ourselves to people who weren't meant to find us.

  • transparency, in the open.

    a public percentage of profits goes to philanthropy. our editorial conflicts are disclosed. our corrections are on the original page.

selva is

  • a curated room of people who care
  • intentional, every detail considered
  • a healthy alternative to slopscrolling
  • a space where you scroll with intention
  • transparent — profits fund philanthropy

selva is not

  • a luxury brand — it's about taste, not wealth
  • a club that exists to keep people out
  • corporate, ad-driven, or algorithm-first
  • silent about where the money goes

philanthropy

a percentage of every dollar goes back out.

this is the part most internet companies leave fuzzy. we're going to keep it plain. membership and marketplace both contribute. the percentage is published publicly. it can only move upward. the causes are voted on quarterly by members on the annual plan.

the honest answers

why a percentage and not a fixed dollar amount?
because we want the allocation to grow with the business, not lag behind it. a fixed number is easy to beat, then sit on. a percentage that can only move up keeps pressure in the right place.
what does "vetted" mean for the causes?
we shortlist organisations with public audited financials, a clear use of funds, and a track record older than two years. members vote from the shortlist.
where is the ledger?
it lives inside the app as a public philanthropy dashboard — amount raised this quarter, allocation percentage, the cause, the receipt. in the journal, we write it up each quarter as a short dispatch.

the founding story

how selva began.

selva was started by jeronimo beckley in late 2025. the short version is that nothing else felt quite right — instagram had become a slot machine, linkedin had become a theatre, substack was great for writers and harder for readers, discord was noisy, geneva was quiet in a different way, and soho house is a building.

the longer version is a decade of notebooks: a list of people jero wanted in one place, a running list of magazines he wished existed, a folder of essays that should have been written. at some point the notebooks stopped being notebooks and became a spec. a year of design, three prototypes, two false starts — and a commitment to ship it carefully rather than fast.

we're a small team. a founding engineering lead, an editor, a community manager, a head of growth. we'll grow slowly, and only when the work requires it. we will not hire a chief engagement officer.

the goal isn't to build a big company. it's to build a room that's worth walking into — and then to keep it that way for a long time.
